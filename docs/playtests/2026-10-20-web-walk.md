## Causes found in the code

**Rails are drawn somewhere other than where they collide** (the largest single cause). `meshpool.rs` drops every `RAILING` primitive from the L2 mesh and draws `l4::railing_modules` instead. That function is from v6: it takes the world AABB of each rail, puts an axis-aligned kit railing at the AABB's middle and bottom with a yaw of 0 or 90°, and sets its length to the longer horizontal side. The module shader (`vs_module`) can only turn by quarter turns about +Y. As a result:
- a rail in a turned lattice is drawn along x or z through its AABB, across the walkway;
- a rail in a tilted lattice is drawn level at the bottom of its AABB, away from its deck, so it floats with no deck under it;
- the flight rail of `edges.rs` is a box plus a `Ramp`, and the ramp is drawn as a level rail at the foot height plus 1.1 m along the whole flight;
- the stepped handrail of `paths_v.rs` is drawn 1.1 m above the handrail.

Collision still uses the real boxes, so the player meets invisible walls where nothing is drawn and walks through rails that are drawn. Shots 1478, 1496, 1538, 1662, 1882, 1969, 1975, 1980, 2011, 2019, 2169 and 2179 all show it.

**Rails take the piece's frame as up.** `edges::deck_edges` and `flight_edges` use the piece frame's +Y for the standing-room test, the drop probe and the rail height. A deck in a tilted or sideways (`Frame::Dir`) lattice is treated as walkable, and its rails stand perpendicular to its surface, so they end up horizontal or leaning.

**Rails cannot see past their own cell.** `edges::World` holds only the L2 cell's own pieces and the parent L1. A deck or flight that continues across an L2 face has nothing beyond its end edge as far as the pass can tell, so that edge counts as open and is railed with chance 0.85. The same happens where a path meets a neighbour's structure or an L3 floor at a door. `Leg::trim` handles corners within one cell only.

**Stair sides interpenetrate.** In `paths_v.rs`, `track` is a solid ramp 0.15 m above the foot whose slope the step corners cross (sawtooth teeth). `ramp` adds a 0.5 m footing under the *whole* run, not just the foot. `handrail` is level slabs of ten units each. In `edges.rs`, `flight_edges`' foot box spans the whole flight at the foot height, so under a free-standing flight it hangs below as a wall.

**Vertical paths do not own their column.** `paths_v::helix` has a 4 m empty core, no rails, and 4 m deck stubs at the ends. Nothing reserves a rise's column against hubs, sets or other paths. `carve` cannot cut cylinders, ramps or stairs, so a round platform in a stair's column stays (1403).

**Dressing without a host.**
- `modifiers.rs` puts MOD-08 lit window panels at `side·(h + 0.45)` beside the path and MOD-04 lamps on "the wall side", whether or not a wall is there.
- `Emitter::lit_faces` places windows per slot face from the slot states, before bores, corridors, entrance cuts and shell insets change the wall.
- `l2_web::drop_in_tram_volumes` keeps every `NO_COLLIDE` piece while it drops the solids they hang on, so windows, lamps and cables outlive their host.
- MOD-06's "doorway frames a lamp" puts a 0.5 m emissive cube on the leg's centre line at its end, inside the next leg's walkway (1980).

**Grid shading is world-aligned.** `scene.wgsl` projects the 1 m and 8 m grid on world planes chosen by the dominant normal. On turned and tilted pieces the 8 m lines cut across floors, walls and ceilings at an angle, so they read as cracks or slices (1779, 1833, 2169).

**Interiors take the lattice tilt.** `WebL3::cell_primitives` turns every block by the cell's full lattice frame, tilt included. In a tilted lattice the floors slope and the blocks clipped to world L3 cells do not meet (1699). The PW.4.1 claim that "inverted and sideways forms keep normal floors" holds only for upright lattices. Interiors also have no L4 modules or lights yet (known, PW.4 handoff).

**Z-fighting across chunks.** `primmesh::nudge` grows turned boxes by 0.15 to 2.4 mm. In an L1 chunk, positions go up to 4 096 m, where one f32 step is about 0.5 mm, so the small nudges are lost. Axis-aligned boxes get no nudge at all. Pieces in different chunks (L1 against L2 or L3) are never culled against each other. Which pieces fight in 1543, 1572 and 1872 is not yet proven: PW.4.7 adds the tool to find out.

**v6 leftovers in the Web pipeline.** `railing_modules` (above); `meshpool::doorway_of` and `hollow_envelope`, which hollow any axis-aligned envelope that a port touches; and `mid_primitives`, which keeps only `ENVELOPE` pieces, so paths and rails disappear past the L2 radius. Each needs checking against the Web rules.

**Performance and streaming.**
- At three of the shot positions the frame rate fell to 6, 33 and 10 fps.
- GPU memory is 1.1 to 2.4 GiB against a 768 MiB budget; the overlay's "over budget" frame count rises every frame.
- Evictions climb by thousands while the camera hovers (2 986 to 10 474 across the session) and rejected chunks rise (33 to 87). The wanted set exceeds the budget, so wanted chunks are evicted and requested again.
- After a jump, the place where you arrive takes seconds to fill.

- The open-topped rings of shot 1512 are a valid form; roofed rings exist as well (FORM-24, PW.4.26).
