# The Web: idea bank (iteration 3)

*Companion to `the-web.md`, 2026-10-18. That file is the architecture; this one is the catalogue of what goes in it. Every entry is a **family**: it gets parameters, variants and mutations, and combines with eras, quarters, regimes, grains and gravity. Each says where it is generated (L0–L5, or engine). Scope stays the same: walking, climbing, drifting and looking, with no combat, inventory or quests.*

---

## 1. Deep time: the vertical axis means something

Absolute depth sets the **era** of a stratum, with noise so the bands are never clean. **Going down is going back in time. Going up is going towards the unfinished.** Every vertical journey becomes a journey through the City's history, and the absurd altimeter numbers start to mean something.

| Era (top to bottom) | Language | Light | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Raw** (highest) | Bare frames, scaffold, unclad floors, cranes, Builders at work, sparks | Work lamps, welding flashes | Huge, half empty |
| **New** | Clean grey panels, glazed skybridges, lit signage nobody reads | Even and cold | Regular, gridded |
| **Concrete** | Brutalist slabs, coffers, ramps, board-marked walls | Sparse sodium | Heavy |
| **Iron** | Rivets, trusses, gasometer frames, chains, gantries | Sepia, furnace glow | Lattice |
| **Warren** | Kowloon accretion, capsules, cables everywhere, human traces | Scattered warm windows | Small, dense |
| **Ceramic** | Smooth white seamless forms, curved corridors, no seams; the Netsphere era | Glowing walls, no lamps | Unsettlingly clean |
| **Fossil** | Older buildings swallowed inside newer mass: stairs into walls, tram cars inside pillars, façades inside halls | Very dark | Mixed |
| **Cyclopean** (deepest) | Monolithic blocks hundreds of metres across, few openings, giant steps no human was meant for | Almost none; your own beacons matter here | Inhuman |

- **Era seams:** where one era sits on another, you see the newer one built over the older. Raw scaffold stands on concrete roofs. Warren capsules cling to iron trusses. A ceramic corridor punches through a cyclopean block.
- **Section cliffs** show the eras stacked like rock strata. A section cliff is a geology lesson made of buildings.
- **Generated** at L0 from absolute y bands plus noise. It feeds every palette below it.

## 2. Wonders: one per regime, visible for 50 km

Each L0 cell (32 km) has a chance of holding one **Wonder**: a unique, enormous structure that is a horizon landmark, so the far promise always has something worth walking to. Wonders are L0 features built from the same primitives. Each has internal routes and a hub. Some candidates:

1. **The Cenotaph:** a hollow sphere 3 km across standing in a hall. Pinholes in its shell show stars of lamp light inside. Inside is a shell gravity well (after Boullée's cenotaph for Newton).
2. **Tatlin's Spiral:** a double-helix lattice 8 km tall, with slowly rotating volumes (a cube, a cylinder, a cone) hanging inside. Each turns once per hour.
3. **The Dam:** a curved wall 6 km long holding back an empty reservoir hall. Walk its crest with a 2 km drop on one side and dry basin on the other, then go down the spillway stair.
4. **The Walking City:** a dead district on legs, a kilometre tall, lying where it stopped (after Archigram). Climb a leg and walk through its belly.
5. **The Tower of Babel, inverted:** a spiral ziggurat 5 km tall hanging from a crust. Its ramp winds down to a point.
6. **The Organ:** a cluster of pipes, each 200–2000 m tall. Wind from a bore makes it sound, so you hear it 20 km away.
7. **The Lamp Sun:** a light so big it has a day. It brightens and dims over 40 minutes, and shadows sweep across a whole Between.
8. **The Loom:** a frame 4 km wide strung with thousands of cables, with Builders shuttling across it.
9. **The Monument:** a perfectly blank slab, 1 km thick and endless, cutting straight through everything for hundreds of kilometres. Gridded white, with no openings except a few gates (after Superstudio's Continuous Monument).
10. **The Hyperboloid:** a Shukhov lattice tower 3 km tall. Its stair runs inside the lattice.
11. **The Wheel:** a ring 2 km across, turning slowly, with cabins that stay level. Ride it to cross 2 km of height.
12. **The Cistern:** a hall of 10 000 identical columns standing in a mirror-still coolant pool (after the Basilica Cistern).
13. **The Drop:** a featherfall shaft 30 km deep through several strata, lit by rings (§8).
14. **The Library:** hexagonal galleries without end, with shelves, spiral stairs and air shafts (after Borges).
15. **The Bridge City:** a span 5 km long across a Between, lined with buildings, shops and chapels on both sides, Ponte Vecchio-style.
16. **The Nest:** a woven chaos of 10 000 beams at every angle around a furnace, like a bird's nest the size of a city.
17. **The Stair:** one grand stair 200 m wide climbing 10 km in a straight line, with landings that are towns.
18. **The Furnace Heart:** a point well whose furnace is visible, a glowing core in a cage of rings.
19. **The Antenna:** a mast 20 km tall with platforms, guy cables kilometres long, and a light at the top that blinks once a minute.
20. **The Archive Wall:** a cliff 3 km tall of identical drawers, each 4 m. Catwalks and ladders cross it everywhere.
21. **The Mirror:** a district duplicated exactly across a plane, down to every lamp.
22. **The Sleeping Builder:** a construction machine kilometres long, frozen mid-task. Its arm holds a beam over a void, and you walk along the arm.
23. **The Funnel:** a conical void 8 km wide at the top, with terraced walls spiralling down to a 50 m hole.
24. **The Gate:** a door in a Rampart (§16.12), 2 km tall, standing ajar.

Wonders are rare: about one per 30–100 km. Each gets a name in the City's glyph script (§15.2).

## 3. Great Roads, Spines and Rivers: features at L0 scale

These are linear features keyed by L0 lines. They run for hundreds of kilometres, so a walker can **follow one forever**.

- **Great Road:** a lit processional route 30–80 m wide. It passes through crusts, across Betweens on bridges, down grand stairs and up ramps. Lamp posts every 50 m, rest niches every 2 km, a sanctuary every 10 km. Variants: the Lantern Road (lit), the Dark Road (lamps dead, with your own beacons as the only light), the Pilgrim Road (worn steps, offerings in niches), the Freight Road (rails and dead cargo).
- **Spine:** a vertical super-artery through every stratum, about one every 30 km. It is a tether with:
  - a lift (a 30 minute ride through Betweens and crusts);
  - a stair that never ends;
  - stations at every crust surface.

  It is the main way to make deep-time journeys.
- **River:** a pipe bundle 300–800 m wide, running hundreds of km with walkways on top and inside. It hums. Variants: the hot river (steam and glow), the dead river (cold and silent), the broken river (sections fallen into a Between, with a detour around them).
- **Rail Abyss:** a tram line across a Between on a single beam with no crust in sight, so the ride is pure void.
- **Cable Highway:** a gondola line slung 20 km across a Between, with swaying cabins and towers on tethers.

## 8. Weightless and low gravity

The free drift verb (decided) opens a whole layer of the world.

- **Featherfall shafts:** shafts with weak gravity, where falling is a slow descent at 1–3 m/s. Drop in, and minutes later land in the next stratum. They are the fast and beautiful way down. Rings of light mark that the shaft is safe. Variants: straight, helical, branching, with platforms to drift between, upward (a rising current).
- **Drift lanes:** tubes and channels in lulls with a current that carries you along. They are rivers in the air between wells.
- **Gravity rivers:** a long visible current, marked by drifting dust and lights, that slowly carries debris and you between two wells.
- **The Archipelago:** a lull the size of a Between where fragments of megastructure float: towers, plazas, a whole street, all turning slowly. Each fragment has its own weak down, so you can stand on them. You drift from one to the next.
- **Tumbler room:** a room whose gravity turns 90° every few minutes, so the furniture is bolted on every wall.
- **Orbiters:** debris, cabins and lamps circling a point well on fixed periods (world clock). Stand on a moon of rubble.
- **Low-gravity districts:** tidal edges where stairs are taken three at a time and falls are gentle. Long drops become possible routes.
- **Weightless cathedral:** a hall of floating lamps, fog and one handrail, with silence.

## 10. Payoffs, batch 2: what you find at the end

- **Anamorphic glyph:** paint across many surfaces, pipes and walls that only forms a coherent symbol from one exact spot, marked by a worn footprint. A perfect reward for a dead end.
- **Cartographer's room:** walls with real maps of the surrounding 30 km, drawn by the generator from its own data, in an old engraved style. They show knots, Wonders, Roads and sanctuaries, and they make the world legible.
- **Focal window:** a window precisely framing a Wonder 40 km away.
- **The previous walker:** a chalk trail of arrows that leads somewhere, hash-placed along a real route, ending at a sanctuary or a view.
- **A lit room in a dark district:** someone kept the lights on. A chair, a book, a cup.
- **Collections:** a room of 400 lamps, of 1000 shoes, of shelved Builder parts. Unsettling and human.
- **Messages:** glyph inscriptions in the City's script (§15.2), consistent across the world, untranslated. Graffiti, plaques, signage, prayers.
- **Echo chamber:** a room with an extraordinary reverb (audio).
- **A seat:** sometimes it is just a bench where the view is.
- **The bell:** a bell in a shaft. Walking through the room rings it, and the sound falls for a long time.

## 11. Light

- **Lighthouses:** knot towers with slow rotating beams visible for 20 km. They are wayfinding in the dark eras, and each has its own rhythm, so you know which one you are looking at.
- **Searchlights** sweeping a Between from crust edges, slow and indifferent.
- **Light rivers:** pulses running along conduits and cables like data, kilometres long, in the Ceramic era.
- **Aurora sheets:** emissive curtains hanging in a Between, rippling.
- **Window weather:** whole districts' windows turning on and off in slow waves.
- **The Lamp Sun's day** (§2.7), and smaller lamp suns over halls.
- **Furnace glow** lighting fog from below.
- **God rays** through a perforated crust from a lamp sun above.
- **Blackout eras**, where your beacons and the wake lights (§13.3) are the only light.
- **Emergency light:** red lamps along evacuation routes in Bastion quarters, leading somewhere.
- **Neon glyph signage** in the New era, unreadable, buzzing.

## 12. Weather and air

All of this is engine-side and deterministic from the world clock and position.

- **Fog sea:** the deepest Betweens hold a cloud layer. Spires and tethers stand out of it, and you can descend into it.
- **Fog tides:** banks drifting slowly across a Between.
- **Condensation rain:** water falling in sheets from cooling pipes high in a Between. Rain inside the world.
- **Dust fall:** grey particles drifting down a shaft forever.
- **Ash snow** from a burning furnace above.
- **Steam vents** on a cycle.
- **Wind** in bores and Roads. Audio, plus dust streams.
- **Frost** near cold machinery: rime on railings, breath visible.
- **Heat haze** near furnaces and hot rivers.
- **Lightning:** arcs along conduits inside a fog sea.
- **Still pools:** coolant mirrors in cisterns and reservoirs, with a planar reflection for that rare set piece.

## 13. Presence: life that is not an enemy

### 13.1 Builders

Machines kilometres long that slowly add to the City. They move on scheduled routes, from the world clock and the generator, so they are the same for everyone, like trams.
- You can watch them place a beam over an hour.
- You can ride one.
- You can find a dock where one is half-built.
- Far away their work lights are slow-moving stars.
- Their additions are a **schedule of predetermined geometry**: the structure exists in the generator already, unbuilt and then built by world time. So the horizon promise holds: what you see built is built.

### 13.2 Machines that notice you

Sensors and old automation, not life:
- an engine hall that starts turning as you walk through;
- lift cars that come when you approach;
- doors opening ahead of you;
- a line of lamps lighting up a kilometre ahead to show the route, then going out behind you.

### 13.3 Wake lights

Dead lights wake as you pass and stay lit for hours of real time. This is engine state, saved like beacons. From a Between overlook you can look back and see **your own trail** across the crust, a thread of light where you walked. It is a quiet record of the journey, and in the dark eras it is beautiful.

### 13.4 Small life

- Drone flocks wheeling in Betweens like starlings.
- Maintenance crawlers on pipes.
- Cable spiders mending nets.
- Dust moths around lamps.

None of them interact; all of them are motion.

### 13.5 Far walkers

Very rarely, a single lantern light on a distant catwalk, moving at walking speed, always going away. You never meet it. Maybe you followed someone's beacons once.

### 13.6 Overgrowth

Biome patches: moss on north faces, hanging roots in wells, pale fungus that glows in the Fossil era, vines in Hydroponic quarters, a whole tree of pipes that is actually a tree.

## 14. Quiet tools, batch 2

### 14.1 Scope

A held zoom that makes the horizon promise tangible. Look at a far structure and **see its detail** (the far band at higher resolution). With **gaze-mark**, pin a far landmark: a faint tone sounds when you face it, and a small mark shows at the screen edge. Telescope rooms in Observatory quarters give a fixed, stronger zoom.

### 14.2 Atlas

Named places, auto-recorded on arrival:
- **What is recorded:** knots, Wonders, Roads, sanctuaries and payoffs.
- **The names:** each name is in the City's glyph script, with a transliteration (`ka-reth vuun`), generated from the place's hash. Every place has a name, and the name is the same for everyone with the seed.
- **The pages:** the Atlas shows each recorded place as a page with the glyph, the altitude, the era and a photo if you took one. It is a journal without quests.

### 14.3 Listening

Hold still to hear a direction, as a stereo hum:
- the nearest sanctuary;
- the furnace;
- the River.

### 14.4 Chalk

Draw a mark on a wall, an arrow or a glyph, saved like beacons. Leave your own trail.

## 15. Variety multipliers

### 15.1 Combination

```
eras (8)  ×  quarters (16)  ×  regimes (5+)  ×  grains (5)  ×  gravity (11 including none)
  × forms (40+)  ×  supports (11)  ×  mutations (14)  ×  paths (80+)  ×  modifiers (9 axes)
```

Not every combination is allowed, but even with heavy filtering this gives millions of distinct neighbourhoods. A form in the Iron era of a Foundry quarter under a line well's gravity is not the same thing as that form in a Ceramic Hospital.

### 15.2 The City's script

A procedural glyph alphabet, fixed per seed, built from strokes on a grid. It is used for names, signage, inscriptions, the Atlas and anamorphic payoffs. It gives the world a language you never learn but come to recognise. Each glyph in a place name is derived from the hash.

### 15.3 Uniqueness rule for hubs

Each knot hub combines a set piece, its quarter, its era and one **rare feature** drawn from a long list (a Wonder fragment, a collection, a lit room, a bell, a mural, a telescope). No two hubs within 30 km share the same combination. That is what "you will find something new there" means in practice.

## 16. Strange regions, batch 2

1. **The Library** (§2.14), as a whole region.
2. **The Recursion:** a region where every structure is made of smaller copies of itself down to 8 m, like a Menger sponge. Self-similarity made literal.
3. **The Inversion:** figure and ground swapped. Strata are void, Betweens are solid, and you walk in tunnels through what should be air.
4. **The Grid:** a lattice of identical 64 m cubes joined by hatches (after *Cube*). Perfectly regular, with every room nearly the same.
5. **Field of Doors:** a hall floor covered in freestanding doorframes.
6. **The Labyrinth:** walls 200 m tall in a true maze, solvable, with a sanctuary at its heart.
7. **Fog district:** visibility of 5 m, navigated by lights and sound.
8. **Silent district:** no machines, no hum, no movers. The absence is the experience.
9. **The Slow district:** everything moves, slowly. Bridges rotate, floors rise and fall over minutes, rooms reconfigure on a cycle. All of it is deterministic, from the world clock.
10. **The Miniature:** a model of the surrounding 10 km at 1:100 on a table in a hall, accurate because the generator made it from its own data. You can find your own position on it.
11. **The Far Lands:** beyond ±10⁶ km on any axis, the eras deepen beyond Cyclopean, the scale grows, and the script changes. Absurd coordinates become a destination.
12. **The Rampart:** the Megastructure wall of BLAME!. An almost impenetrable wall 5 km thick that separates super-regions every ~300 km. It is seen from 50 km away as the edge of the world. Crossing it happens only at rare Gates (§2.24) or through the Drop.
13. **Shattered grain**, **Möbius corridor**, **section cliff**, **quiet lull**, **planetoid** (from the-web.md §12).
14. **Builder frontier:** at the top of the Raw era, the edge where the City is still being made. Construction lights to the horizon. There is no top; there is only the frontier.

## 18. Sound (design only; audio is deferred)

- **The City's breath:** a deep cycle in the drone, minutes long, felt rather than heard.
- **Horns:** a tram horn crossing a Between echoes for 20 seconds.
- **Stress:** beams groaning in the cold, like whales.
- **Wind organs:** bores whistle at a pitch set by their length, so each bore has its own note.
- **The Organ Wonder,** heard from 20 km.
- **Bells** in shafts, falling sound.
- **Choir resonance** in stair cathedrals, from your own footsteps.
- **Sanctuary chimes;** radio murmurs in bunk rooms.
- **Silence zones,** which are the most powerful sound.
